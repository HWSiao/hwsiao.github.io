Abstract
======
The Sun emits broadband radiation across all electromagnetic wave bands, and is the predominant source of space weather through sudden activities such as solar flares or eruptions. These events can accelerate particles to exceptionally high energies, sending them through the solar corona into the interplanetary space as well as Earth’s vicinity. For accelerated electrons, they can emit radio emission through diverse mechanisms, such as gyromagnetic emission or plasma emission, depending on the ambient plasma medium into which these electron beams are propagated. Consequently, the study of solar radio emission properties offers a powerful tool for remotely diagnosing the conditions of electron beams, including processes like acceleration, injection, and propagation, as well as insights into the
medium itself. These insights have the potential to improve space weather detection.  
In this presentation, I will discuss the recent studies focusing on one category for solar radio emission—type III radio bursts. These studies use data from the Parker Solar Probe (PSP), a recent spacecraft mission that provides high-cadence space-based radio measurements in the inner heliosphere. Additionally, I will provide a brief overview of recent developments in solar radio observations related to Taiwan space weather.
