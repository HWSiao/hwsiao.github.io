Abstract
======
Earth’s magnetic field results from the superposition of signals originating from various sources. The most significant variations result from geomagnetic storms, which are intense disturbances in Earth's magnetosphere caused by a powerful transfer of energy from the solar wind into the space environment surrounding our planet. In addition to geomagnetic storms, ground-based measurements occasionally exhibit smooth variations depending on local time, typically in the order of a few tens of nanoteslas (nT). This phenomenon is attributed to Solar quiet (Sq) currents, a type of electric current flowing in the sunlit side of the ionosphere. Although Sq current rise up a relatively small amplitude of geomagnetic field than storms and substorms, usually around several hundred nT at ground level, studies on Sq currents are crucial for advancing our understanding of ionospheric electrodynamics and for monitoring local space weather conditions.
This study conducted a thorough examination of local geomagnetic field variations in Taiwan spanning from 1965, encompassing solar cycles 20 to 25 (1964-2024). Geomagnetic data from July 1965 to December 2000 were collected at Lunping Observatory (LNP, 25.0°N, 121.17°E), situated about 14° north of the magnetic equator. Notably, the study involved digitizing booklets containing original geomagnetic field records from July 1965 to December 1993. The data from the last decade were obtained from 12 ground stations established across Taiwan by the Taiwan Geophysical Networks for Seismology (TGNS) and the Central Weather Administration (CWA) geomagnetic observatories. Our study provides a broader chronological coverage compared to data archived at the World Data Center (WDC) for Geomagnetism. The objective of this study is to uncover potential local geomagnetic influences in historical data and provide a basis for assessing future local impacts of space weather.
