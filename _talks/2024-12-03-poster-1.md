Abstract
======
Equatorial plasma bubbles (EPBs) are regions within the ionosphere where plasma density is significantly lower than that of the surrounding areas. These EPBs can cause disruptions to GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) signals, affecting both the phase and amplitude of the signals. This interference results in errors in positioning accuracy, a phenomenon commonly referred to as ionospheric scintillations. Due to its unique geographic location near the magnetic equator, Taiwan is particularly susceptible to experiencing EPBs. However, accurately detecting the locations of these EPBs is a complex task at both regional and global scales.  
To overcome these challenges, the Space Weather Operational Office (SWOO), established by the Central Weather Administration (CWA), has the main objective of enhancing space weather information and providing local space weather forecasts. For ionospheric products, SWOO employs data from both space-based and ground-based sources. To verify the locations of plasma bubbles, SWOO uses bubble geolocation data from FORMOSAT-7/COSMIC-2 measurements and ionospheric scintillation data from GNSS ground receivers operated by the CWA/Seismological Center. By combining these two types of observations, it is possible to trace the movement of EPBs in the areas surrounding Taiwan. This method is probably able to be a useful tool provided the information for the local monitor of space weather.
