Abstract
======
To mitigate response time latencies and address geographical differences in global space weather notifications, the Central Weather Administration (CWA) has developed near real-time, automated monitoring data pipelines within its operational infrastructure since April 2020. CWA is Taiwan's official space weather authority, and its primary goal is to enhance the local monitoring capabilities and provide accurate, credible and systematic space weather information with minimal latency.  

As part of expanding local solar activity and space radiation observation capabilities, CWA actively collaborates with domestic academic institutes on the e-CALLISTO (Compound Astronomical Low frequency Low cost Instrument for Spectroscopy and Transportable Observatory) solar radio spectrometer network, a core instrument initiative under the International Space Weather Initiative (ISWI) framework. Solar radio bursts (SRBs) observed by e-CALLISTO provide crucial real-time diagnostics of particle acceleration and shock propagation associated with solar flares and CMEs, serving as vital precursors to radio blackouts and solar energetic particle (SEP) events.  

In Taiwan, the TAIWAN-NCU e-CALLISTO station was established in January 2022 at the National Central University (NCU) Chung-Li VHF Radar Station, co-maintained by the Solar Activity and Geospace Exploration (SAGE) Laboratory. However, because the TAIWAN-NCU station frequently experiences radio frequency interference (RFI), CWA plans to establish a new e-CALLISTO station at the Hengchun Weather Station (22.00°N, 120.74°E), which is the southernmost weather station in Taiwan and offers an exceptionally low RFI background. This new station is projected to be fully established and integrated into operational service by 2027.  

While hardware instrumentation for e-CALLISTO is well established globally, traditional event classification and spectroscopic measurement evaluation heavily rely on manual inspection. To streamline this process for operational space weather monitoring, CWA is developing an automated, AI-driven detection and classification framework. Trained and validated using continuous radio observations from the TAIWAN-NCU e-CALLISTO station, this system aims to automatically identify and classify key types of solar radio bursts (e.g., Type II and Type III bursts) in near real time.  

This presentation introduces CWA current operational automation infrastructure for solar activity and space radiation monitoring. We highlight the performance of the AI-driven solar radio burst classification system, and present the operational roadmap to fully transition these AI research outcomes into CWA official forecasting pipeline by 2027, thereby enhancing early warning capabilities for solar eruptive events.
