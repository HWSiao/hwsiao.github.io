Abstract
======
Ionospheric variations in the equatorial and low-latitude regions are highly dynamic, disrupting critical infrastructure such as high-frequency and satellite communications, as well as degrading the performance of navigation and positioning services. The Space Weather Operational Office (SWOO), established by the Central Weather Administration (CWA) in Taiwan, has utilized radio occultation (RO) products from the FORMOSAT-7/COSMIC-2 (F7/C2) satellite constellation since 2019, to provide various space weather monitoring products and enhance forecasting capabilities. Comprising six small satellites in a 24-degree inclination orbit at an altitude of approximately 520 km, the F7/C2 constellation provides high-density data coverage across mid- and low-latitudes, offering high temporal and spatial resolution ionospheric observations through the TriG GNSS Radio Occultation System (TGRS). The operational products provided by SWOO include near-real-time 6-hour electron density profiles, global foF2 and hmF2 maps, and scintillation index (S4) maps. Furthermore, RO data are integrated with ground-based GNSS Total Electron Content (TEC) measurements to construct the Taiwan Realtime Global (TWRG) TEC map and the Global Ionospheric Specification (GIS) reanalysis product. These observations are also assimilated into models to enhance global ionospheric nowcasting and forecasting capabilities. This presentation will detail these operational applications to highlight the critical role of small satellite constellations with low inclination orbit in advancing ionospheric space weather monitoring and filling the observational gaps over equatorial regions.
