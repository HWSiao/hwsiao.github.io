Abstract
======
Accurate localization of equatorial plasma bubbles (EPBs) represents a significant challenge for space weather services in the low-latitude ionosphere. To address these challenges, Center Weather Administration (CWA) employs data from both space-based and ground-based sources to monitor EPBs traveling the Taiwan region. This study evaluates characteristics of EPBs using these several following observations: (1) FORMOSAT-7/COSMIC-2 (F7/C2) products, including scintillation geolocation data, Radio Occultation (RO) profiles, and in-situ ion density from the Ion Velocity Meter (IVM); (2) amplitude scintillation indices (S4) derived from the dense Taiwan GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) ground-based receiver network; (3) OI 630.0 nm airglow emissions captured by the full-band chromatic all-sky imager at Lulin observatory (23.5°N, 120.9°E, 12.5°N magnetic latitude); and (4) spread-F signatures and F-layer height variations detected by the ionosonde at the Xinwu weather station (25.0oN, 121.0oE, 16.0°N magnetic latitude).  
Combined with these diverse observational techniques, this study shows the capability to track the movement of EPBs with cross-validation between space-based and ground-based observations, offering a valuable methodology for local space weather monitoring and forecasting services.
